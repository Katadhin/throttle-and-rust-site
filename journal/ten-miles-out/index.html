Caleb The foothills, NC

Ten Miles Out

The trailer tire let go about ten miles out, loud enough that I had us on the shoulder before I was done deciding to brake. The '78 was strapped down tight behind us and didn't shift, which is the one thing that went right all afternoon.

Got out into the smoke and the gravel dust. Sarah was already around back looking at it. Shredded down to the belts, nothing you drive on. Pulled the spare off the rack and it was flat too. Flat since who knows when, eight dollars of air I had meant to check on Tuesday and didn't, the kind of thing you don't find out you skipped until you're standing on the side of a road finding it out.

Hot laps were in an hour. We were not making hot laps.

Sarah kicked the blown tire and laughed, not the polite kind, the kind that means we will be telling this one at the shop for a while. Nobody to call. You either carry what you need to fix your own stuff or you learn something about yourself on the shoulder of a two-lane. Texted Wes. A jack and a good spare whenever you're loose. He sent back a thumbs up and give me forty.

So we dropped the tailgate on the tow rig instead of sitting in the cab feeling sorry about it. Two cans out of the dented cooler in the bed, still cold enough to count. Crank climbed down and found himself a spot in the grass like he had planned the stop all along. Cars came by close enough to move your hair, and we sat there and went back through every bad night we own. The motor that let go right on the grid with everybody watching. The year it rained so hard they called it before the feature and we ate cold chicken in the truck bed until midnight anyway.

Wes rolled up right around the forty-minute mark, faded work shirt, in no hurry. Grabbed a can before he looked at the tire. Leaned on the trailer and said it would keep, which it did. We changed it eventually. Nobody rushed it.

Never turned a lap. Never got closer than that gravel shoulder. First Saturday in a month I was not running the list of everything still left to do on the car, and I have got a ruined spare to thank for it.
