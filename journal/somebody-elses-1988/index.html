Somebody Else's 1988

Everybody came down for the weekend. Daddy and Mama drove over Saturday, Wes came Sunday, the Meg got here late with wine nobody needed and everybody drank. The table came out of the rafters Friday and did not go back until Tuesday.

The pot went on Sunday afternoon and sat there doing what it does, which is nothing anybody can rush. Daddy checked it twice in six hours and stirred it neither time.

Wore the shirt. Faded gray, red letters, the same one out of the same drawer. It does not fit any better than it did in May.

The cars ran Sunday night in old paint. That is the thing they do at that track over Labor Day, teams putting schemes from thirty and forty years back on cars that have nothing else in common with them. Colors that were on every hauler in the lot when Daddy was running. Big blocks of orange and yellow, numbers in a font nobody uses anymore, sponsors that have not existed since before I could drive.

Daddy went quiet. Not upset quiet. The other one, where he is somewhere else in his head and you leave him there.

Asked him about it during a commercial. He said the colors were right and the rest of it was not, and that was as far as he went. Pressed him a little. He said it looked like a costume of a thing he used to wear to work.

Mama, from her chair, not looking up: it was never that pretty when you were doing it. He said no it was not. That was the closest anybody came to talking about it.

The thing about a throwback is somebody decided your decade was worth selling back. Which is a compliment, and it is also a business, and Daddy has been around long enough to know both those things are true at once and to not need to say which one bothers him.

My shirt is from 1988 too. Four races, a team that folded inside the year, a rubber band around the box of stuff that came home with it. Nobody put that on a car. Nobody is going to. It went from his closet to my drawer and it comes out three or four times a year and goes back, and that is the entire distribution.

Which is the part that keeps. Not the paint. The paint is somebody else's now and there is money in it and that is fine. But there is a whole layer under the part worth selling, guys who ran mid-pack on Saturday nights and went to work Monday, and that layer does not get a scheme. It gets a drawer in a house in Caldwell County and a daughter who keeps putting the shirt on.

He left Monday morning before the traffic. Told me the chili was better than last time. It was the same as last time.
