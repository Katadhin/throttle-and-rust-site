Coming Back for It

Ricky and Ella Mae drove home to Wilkesboro Sunday night a little after dark, and the chili pot did not go with them. It was sitting on the cold burner in the side yard when I went out this morning, lid on, right where he left it.

He called Sarah before eight to say he forgot it. Sarah told him she would keep it. Ella Mae got on the line just long enough to say that man has not forgotten one thing since 1979, then handed the phone back.

That pot has been to every race weekend this family has had since Sarah talked him into cooking again. It rides home on the floorboard where he can reach it. I have never seen it farther from him than the length of a ladle.

Somebody had to wash it, so I did. Ricky has rules about cast iron, and he has told me every one of them more than once. Hot water and a stiff brush. No soap, not ever. Dry it over a flame and rub a little oil in while it is still warm, or don't bother touching it.

So I carried it around to the spigot with a kettle of hot water from the kitchen. There was fog lying in the river bottom, and the color has come about halfway down the ridge since Friday. Crank came out and lay down next to Ricky's chair, which nobody had folded up either.

It took the better part of half an hour. That iron has been black since before I met Sarah, and you don't scrub it so much as talk it into letting go of Sunday.

Here is what I think. A man that particular about a pot does not forget it. Ricky doesn't say much out loud that he means. He was never going to call and say he would be back soon. He leaves something where you will have to look at it.

Sarah figured it out before I did. She said he left a jacket at her apartment in Boone the first fall she was away at school, and he drove up the mountain three weekends running and never once remembered to take it home.

Dried it over the burner, oiled it, and set it on the garage bench next to the Bustelo can, where it is the first thing anybody sees walking in. I didn't put it in a cabinet. It is not ours to put away.

Most people tell you they'll be back. Ricky leaves the pot.
