Sarah Happy Valley, NC

Downriver

The ridge behind us went over in about two weeks this year. The top turned first, the way it always does, and then the color walked down the slope a little more every morning until it reached the trees along the river. Now the crown is gray branches, and the rest is holding on out of stubbornness.

Everybody watches that part. People drive up the hill on a Saturday to watch it. Nobody talks much about where it all goes afterward.

It goes in the river. After lunch I took a cup of coffee down to the flat rock at the back edge, the one Caleb set the dry-stack wall beside in July, and sat there long enough for the coffee to go cold. The Yadkin was carrying leaves the whole time. Not a flood of them, just a steady supply. Red ones, rust ones, a few yellow, turning slow in the eddy behind the big rock and then letting go and moving on.

Crank came down after me and lay at the foot of the sycamore. He did not watch the leaves. The duck did. It got in upstream, rode the current about twenty feet with great dignity, climbed out, walked back up the bank, and did it again. I lost count around six.

Growing up in Wilkesboro, I knew this river from the other end. There is a bridge there I used to drop sticks off of with Mama, and once I asked her where the river came from. She said, "Up there," and pointed in no particular direction, which was as far as her interest in the subject went.

I live up there now. That took me a while to put together.

Everything that falls in our yard this week is headed her way. Not quickly, and not all of it. Some of it catches on the first rock and stays the winter. But the river runs one direction, and it runs toward Wilkesboro, and it has been doing that the entire time I've been worried about which town is home.

I called her from the rock. Told her the river was full of leaves.

"Ours too," she said.

"Those are mine," I said.

She thought about it. "Well, come get them, then."

So we're going up Sunday.
