Sarah Blowing Rock, NC

Up the Hill

Sunday belonged to the race and everybody who came for it. The pie went in at four, the porch filled up, and by the time the green flag dropped there was no version of that day that was about my dad. He did not mind. He never minds. That is half the reason you want to give him a day.

So we saved it for Monday.

Drove him up the hill to Blowing Rock, where the air drops ten degrees and the morning feels borrowed from somewhere cooler. Caleb rode shotgun. My dad sat in the back with his cap on, telling a story Caleb has heard before, and Caleb let him tell it like it was new. That is its own kind of gift.

They put us out on the patio at Famous Toastery. Red umbrellas, clipped hedges, a little iron chiminea standing cold in the corner, the whole ridge laid out behind us going soft and green. The coffee came in thick white mugs that hold their heat. My dad wrapped both hands around his the way he has done at every kitchen table I can remember.

He ordered the breakfast he has ordered my whole life. Eggs, a biscuit the size of a fist, the potatoes. No studying the menu, no deliberating. He knows what he likes, and he has known it longer than I have been alive.

Caleb ate half of mine, which is its own tradition by now, and somewhere in the last fifteen years I stopped pretending to mind. There is a short list of things you let go of once you have been married long enough, and a man reaching across the table for your plate is near the top of it.

We did not talk about much. The weather. Whether the truck will ever run right. Whether the duck has gotten worse, which it has. My dad does not need a conversation steered. He is content to sit somewhere good with his people and let a morning go at its own pace.

A day late, it turns out, is not late at all. It is only quieter. We had the patio nearly to ourselves, and the ridge did the rest, and when his cup ran empty he asked if there was time for one more. There was. There is almost always time for one more cup with him, and I am learning to hold onto that while it is still true.
